# Workplace Hygiene Guide

Workplace hygiene involves maintaining clean, comfortable, and well-organized business environments through regular cleaning and responsible workplace practices.

## Important Hygiene Areas

Businesses should regularly consider:

* Workstations
* Reception areas
* Meeting rooms
* Kitchens
* Restrooms
* Common areas
* Entrances
* Shared equipment
* High-touch surfaces

## High-Touch Surfaces

Frequently handled surfaces may require regular attention.

Examples include:

* Door handles
* Light switches
* Handrails
* Elevator buttons
* Cabinet handles
* Taps
* Countertops
* Shared equipment
* Reception desks
* Meeting-room tables

The appropriate cleaning frequency depends on usage and workplace requirements.

## Employee Hygiene Practices

Employees can contribute to a cleaner workplace by:

1. Washing their hands regularly.
2. Disposing of waste correctly.
3. Keeping workstations organized.
4. Cleaning shared areas after use.
5. Reporting spills promptly.
6. Following workplace hygiene procedures.

## Kitchen Hygiene

Shared kitchens and break rooms should receive regular attention. Countertops, sinks, tables, appliance surfaces, handles, floors, and waste areas can be incorporated into routine cleaning schedules.

## Restroom Hygiene

Restrooms should be cleaned according to their occupancy and usage. Routine tasks may include cleaning toilets, sinks, mirrors, floors, taps, handles, and other frequently touched surfaces.

## Building a Hygiene Routine

A workplace hygiene program should consider:

* Number of occupants
* Traffic levels
* Business type
* Operating hours
* Shared facilities
* Cleaning frequency
* Surface materials

Consistent cleaning combined with responsible workplace habits can help maintain a professional environment.
